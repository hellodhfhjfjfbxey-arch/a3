Note-Taking Methods

Abstract. Good notes are not a copy of the lecture. They are a tool for thinking and, later, for testing yourself.

The Studies editors · November 2026 · 5 sections

1. Why method matters

Writing everything down leaves no room to think. A method gives structure: it forces you to select, summarise and organise, which is where learning happens.

Note 1Thinking happens when you choose what to write.

2. The Cornell method

Divide the page into a wide notes column, a narrow cue column and a summary strip at the bottom. Take notes during class, add questions in the cue column afterwards and write a short summary. Cover the notes and answer the cues to revise.

Note 2Cues turn notes into a quiz.

3. Outlines and mind maps

  • Outline: headings and indented points, ideal for structured lectures.
  • Mind map: a central topic with branches, ideal for connections and brainstorming.
Note 3Pick the method that matches the lecture.

4. Charting

When comparing several things across the same features, such as theories, periods or experiments, draw a table. Rows are items, columns are features. Gaps in the table show what you still need to find out.

Note 4Tables reveal missing information.

5. Review within 24 hours

Whatever the method, revisit notes within a day. Fill gaps, highlight key terms sparingly and turn the main points into questions. Notes reviewed early are remembered far better.

Note 5A quick review saves hours later.
