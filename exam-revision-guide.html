Exam Revision Plan

Abstract. A calm, six-week plan that spreads the work, builds memory through practice and leaves the last days for rest and review.

The Studies editors · November 2026 · 5 sections

1. Make a map of everything

List every subject and topic you need to cover, using the syllabus or course outline. Rate each topic red, amber or green by how confident you feel. This map shows where your time should go.

Note 1Use the syllabus as your checklist.

2. Build a weekly timetable

Block out fixed commitments first, then add study sessions of 30 to 50 minutes with short breaks. Rotate subjects through the week rather than spending a whole day on one, and leave one evening free.

Note 2Shorter sessions keep focus sharp.

3. Revise actively

  • Test yourself with past papers and flashcards.
  • Explain topics aloud without notes.
  • Turn notes into questions, then answer them a day later.
  • Mark what you got wrong and return to it.
Note 3Effort while recalling is the point.

4. Practise under exam conditions

From week four, do timed past papers. Practise reading questions carefully, planning answers and managing time. Review your marked answers: they show exactly what to revise next.

Note 4Mark schemes reveal what examiners want.

5. The last week

Stop learning new material. Review summaries, redo questions you found hard and sleep well. The night before, pack your things, glance over key points and rest. Tired brains recall less.

Note 5Sleep consolidates memory.
